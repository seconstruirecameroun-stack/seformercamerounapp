# Les bassins hydrographiques du Cameroun — script verrouillé

Format : CM1, 16:9, 6 plans.
Voix : Céline, articulation claire, débit posé. Niba explique à l’écran.

Fil conducteur : On remonte un fleuve : d’abord l’eau, puis les régions qu’elle traverse.

## Narration

1. L’hydrographie, c’est l’ensemble des cours d’eau d’une région.
2. Un bassin est la région arrosée par un fleuve et ses affluents.
3. Un affluent se jette dans un fleuve plus grand pour le grossir.
4. Le Cameroun compte quatre bassins : le Tchad, le Niger, l’Atlantique et le Congo.
5. Le Logone arrose Maroua, et la Bénoué arrose Garoua.
6. La Sanaga rejoint l’océan, et la Sangha coule dans la forêt.

## Ce qui est à l’image

1. The outline of Cameroon seen from high above, narrow and pointed at the north, wide in the south; many small blue streams drawing themselves across it, morning light.
2. One wide river in a valley with several smaller rivers joining it from the hills, and a soft tinted area spreading outward to show the land this river waters.
3. A narrow river flowing into a much wider river, the two waters meeting, the wide river visibly swelling and continuing thicker than before.
4. The map of Cameroon with four basins tinted in their real places — Chad at the northern tip, Niger around Garoua, Atlantic across the centre and south, Congo in the far south-east corner — and four arrows showing where the water finally goes.
5. Split view, both in the real north. Left: the real Sahel of the Far North of Cameroon: flat pale ochre plain, dusty pale sky, sparse flat-topped thorny acacias, rounded granite domes of the Mandara hills, conical earth houses with pointed thatch caps, millet granaries, a humped zebu, with the Logone running north past Maroua. Right: the real Sudanian savannah of the North of Cameroon: tall golden grass, broad scattered trees, a wide river showing pale sandbanks, wooden pirogues at a river landing, with the Bénoué turning west at Garoua.
6. Split view. Left: the real forest of southern Cameroon: dense green rainforest, oil palms, red laterite earth, a wide brown river, the Sanaga widening towards the Atlantic with mangroves and the dark cone of Mount Cameroon on the horizon. Right: the real equatorial forest of eastern Cameroon: very dense dark forest, very tall emergent trees, slow still black water under deep shade, the Sangha flowing slowly under the trees.
